6. Theoretical Challenges & Project Conclusion

Critical synthesis of the ontological and computational bottlenecks encountered during the semantic enrichment of complex iconographical assets.

Challenge 1

The Granularity Gap in Cultural Ontologies

The authoritative ICCD/ArCo ontological framework exhibits an expressive limitation when dealing with multi-actor narrative canvases. While ArCo provides robust properties to define an artwork's material, authorship, and broad cultural classification, it lacks spatialized fine-grained primitives to express internal spatial topology.

// Expressive Compromise:
To model Sandro Botticelli's 9 core figures without altering the core schema, we had to rely on overloading core:hasPart (traditionally used for physical components like frames or predellas) and linking them to generic a-cd:Subject nodes. This demonstrates that current cultural heritage standards treat complex visual narratives as singular monolithic units rather than semantic networks.
Challenge 2

Ontological Inversion & Hallucination Risks

Deploying Large Language Models (LLMs) as autonomous Knowledge Engineers introduces significant structural vulnerabilities. Our prompting tiers proved that as semantic complexity and logical constraints increase (e.g., forcing grid calculations in Chain-of-Thought), the models' focus on strict graph directionality degrades.

// Empirical Failure Log:
• Zero-Shot Tier: Total failure in namespace resolution, with both models hallucinating fake local URIs due to lack of contextual anchoring.
• Chain-of-Thought Tier: Gemini 1.5 Pro successfully resolved advanced iconographical labels (the humanistic names of the Graces) but introduced a critical Ontological Inversion Bug, swapping the direction of the predicate (Component core:hasPart Artwork).

// Human-in-the-Loop Curation Verdict

The results of this project demonstrate that while LLMs are incredibly powerful for accelerating the ingestion and conceptual organization of unstructured historical metadata, they cannot operate as fully autonomous Semantic Web publishers. The high sensitivity of graph databases to broken namespaces or inverted predicates means that a single model error can completely isolate a dataset from the global Linked Open Data cloud.

Final Project Takeaway: Our progressive prompting workflow shows that a hybrid framework—leveraging Few-Shot structural examples to lock in namespaces, Chain-of-Thought reasoning to extract complex hidden layouts, and a final Human-in-the-Loop reconciliation layer—is currently the most robust and scalable method for bridging data omissions in cultural heritage knowledge graphs.
